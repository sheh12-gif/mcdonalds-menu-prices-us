What appears on the Sweets and Treats menu

The official Sweets and Treats category groups several dessert styles. McFlurry orders combine soft serve with a mix in, while a Vanilla Cone is a simpler soft serve choice. Sundaes add a topping, shakes are sold by size and bakery items such as pies or cookies have separate ingredient statements. This range makes the menu useful for different cravings, but it does not create one shared calorie or allergen profile.

The homepage references in the table cover a selection rather than every possible flavor, size or limited time item. A regular McFlurry and a Mini McFlurry are different portions. A shake can have small, medium and large options. Check the exact name and size shown on the local menu before comparing either price or nutrition.

How to compare dessert prices and calories

The price list uses homepage amounts, which are reference values rather than a promise for every restaurant. The nutrition table uses the official regular OREO McFlurry page. It should not be applied to a Mini size, another mix in, a sundae or a shake. Soft serve, cookies, syrups and toppings each bring different ingredients and nutrients.

For a fair comparison, start with serving size and product type. The price for a regular McFlurry is not directly comparable to a single cookie or cone without also considering portion and add ins. If your local app offers a different size or seasonal flavor, open that exact product listing. Standard nutrition values may change when ingredients or serving portions change.

Ingredients and personal allergy checks

The regular OREO McFlurry combines vanilla reduced fat ice cream with OREO cookie pieces. The official ingredient statements list milk in the ice cream and wheat and soy in the cookie pieces. Other sweets have different recipes. In particular, a milk based soft serve product is not the same ingredient set as a baked pie or cookie.

An allergen chart for one dessert cannot safely describe every item in the category. Use the current product page for the flavor and size you plan to order. McDonald’s also warns that shared equipment and preparation areas can cause contact with other foods. If an allergy is involved, confirm with the restaurant and do not treat a not listed status as a guarantee.

Ingredients and how the item is built

The regular OREO McFlurry is made from vanilla reduced fat ice cream blended with OREO cookie pieces. The ice cream ingredient statement lists milk, sugar, cream and corn syrup, along with stabilizers and flavoring. The cookie statement lists enriched wheat flour, sugar, palm oil, cocoa and other ingredients. The official page provides the detailed supplier list for this specific serving.

A Mini McFlurry is available as a separate size, and other treats use different ingredients. A sundae includes a topping, a shake uses a flavored base, and a pie has a pastry shell. Always match the nutrition and allergen information to the full product name and size rather than assuming that all desserts share the same formula.

Allergen information

For the regular OREO McFlurry, the official component statements identify milk in the ice cream and wheat and soy in the cookie pieces. Other major allergens are marked not listed in the reviewed product statements. This does not promise an absence of shared kitchen contact, and the statuses do not apply to other Sweets and Treats products.
